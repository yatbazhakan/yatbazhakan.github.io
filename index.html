04 - Selected work

Plate I · ACM TIST 2025 · 3D LiDAR Introspection

Multi-Layer Self-Assessment

Filtering and cross-layer activation monitoring for 3D object detection

Deep 3D object detectors can fail silently when encountering adverse weather, sparse point clouds, or out-of-distribution road scenes. Instead of relying on a single late feature map or miscalibrated confidence scores, this framework extracts activation signatures across multiple backbone depths and applies spatial ego-corridor filtering so the safety monitor focuses on hazards that matter to the vehicle's trajectory.

Plate II · IEEE T-ITS 2024 · Survey & Taxonomy

Introspection of DNN Perception

State-of-the-art and open research challenges in automated driving

How can an automated vehicle tell that its deep neural perception pipeline is making a critical mistake without ground-truth labels at run time? This work synthesises the field of DNN introspection across 2D/3D detection and segmentation, formalising failure triggers, evaluation metrics, and the latency–reliability trade-offs required for safety-critical deployment.

Plate III · IEEE T-IV 2024 & CVPRW 2024 · Activation Representations

Early-Layer & Representation Introspection

Detecting 2D and 3D perception errors before final-layer suppression

Prior introspection methods almost exclusively inspect the penultimate layer of a detector—where features of missed objects have already been attenuated. Across our IEEE T-IV and CVPRW SAIAD papers, we demonstrated that early-layer neural activations and learned representation embeddings capture rich failure cues while cutting monitoring latency.

Real-World Autonomy · Queen's University Belfast

From Neural Activations to Road-Ready Trust

Bridging theoretical AI safety and physical autonomous platforms

At Queen's University Belfast (School of EEECS), our research connects foundational advances in deep neural network introspection with real-world multi-modal sensing platforms. By monitoring internal activation dynamics across LiDAR, camera, and cooperative V2X streams, we aim to build autonomous systems that continuously quantify their own perceptual reliability in complex, everyday environments.

For hosts

Dr. Hakan Yekta Yatbaz is a Lecturer (Assistant Professor) in Autonomous Systems in the School of Electronics, Electrical Engineering and Computer Science (EEECS) at Queen's University Belfast. His research focuses on safe, reliable, and trustworthy AI for autonomous systems, spanning robust multi-modal sensing, run-time monitoring and introspection, explainable representations, and cooperative autonomy. Before joining Queen's, he was a Postdoctoral Research Fellow at WMG, University of Warwick, where he worked on the EU-funded EVENTS and Hi-Drive automated driving projects and completed his Ph.D. in Engineering. He holds B.Sc. and M.Sc. degrees in Computer Engineering from Middle East Technical University (METU NCC) and serves as an Associate Editor for Frontiers in Future Transportation.
